Tumblebloom privacy policy

Last updated 20 November 2026

Tumblebloom is a wordless puzzle game made by Oddly Clever. This page explains what the game keeps, what it shares, and what you can do about it. We've tried to write it the way we'd want it explained to us.

The short version

  • We don't collect anything about you. There are no accounts, no analytics and no servers of ours. Your progress stays on your device.
  • On Android, ads come from Google AdMob. Google receives some device data to show ads and stop fraud. We ask Google for non-personalised ads for every player, so ads are not targeted at you based on your activity.
  • Purchases on Android go through Google Play. We never see your payment details.
  • On CrazyGames, CrazyGames runs the ads and its own privacy policy applies.
  • Tumblebloom is for all ages. On Android it asks for your year of birth once; players under 13 only get ads that are suitable for children.

What the game stores on your device

To remember your progress, Tumblebloom saves a small file on your device:

  • levels unlocked and stars earned
  • hints, sunshine and garden progress
  • your Daily Bloom results and streak
  • your sound setting and chosen outfit
  • a record of what you've bought (the product name and Google's purchase reference), so purchases work when you're offline
  • on Android, the year of birth you entered on the first screen, so the game knows which ads are suitable

On Android this file lives in the app's private storage and is not included in device backups. In a web browser it lives in the browser's storage for the site you play on. None of it is sent to us. If you play on CrazyGames while signed in to CrazyGames, CrazyGames may also keep a copy of this save with your CrazyGames account so your progress follows you; see their policy below.

Ads on Android: Google AdMob

The Android version shows ads through Google AdMob, with Google's consent tool (the User Messaging Platform). Ads are optional rewards (for example, a hint or five extra moves) or a short break between levels. There is always a free way to keep playing.

When the game requests an ad, Google's software on your device collects and shares with Google:

  • your IP address, which can give an approximate location
  • device identifiers, such as the advertising ID and app set ID
  • app interactions, such as app launches, taps and ad views
  • diagnostic information, such as how the ads performed

Players under 13. If the year of birth shows you may be under 13 (we count the year you turn 13 as under 13), every ad request from your device is marked as directed to children and as under the age of consent. Google then shows only ads suitable for children, never personalised, and the advertising ID is not sent.

Google uses this to show ads, to measure them and to prevent fraud. It is encrypted in transit. We have set every ad request to non-personalised ads only and to Google's most family-friendly ad rating (G). We don't receive this data ourselves; AdMob only gives us totals, such as how many ads were shown.

Google's consent form (shown in the European Economic Area and the UK) lists the ad technology partners Google works with. Google may share ad data with them under its own policies; the form lets you see and refuse them.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland or a US state with its own privacy law, Google's consent form appears before any ad. You can change your answer at any time under the gear on the map, then Privacy choices.

Read more: Google Privacy Policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Purchases on Android: Google Play

Remove ads, hint packs and outfits are sold through Google Play's billing system. Google handles the payment; we never see your card or payment details. Each time the game starts, it asks Google Play which items you own, so a purchase comes back if you reinstall. Google Play's own privacy policy covers your purchase data.

Playing on the web

  • CrazyGames. CrazyGames hosts the game, shows the ads and handles any data that involves, under the CrazyGames privacy policy. The game itself sends nothing to us.
  • Other sites (such as itch.io). That version has no ads and no purchases. The site that hosts it may keep ordinary visit logs under its own privacy policy.

Android permissions

  • Internet and network state: to load ads and talk to Google Play.
  • Vibration: for small haptic taps while you play.
  • Advertising ID: used by Google's ads software, as described above.
  • Billing: for Google Play purchases.

The game does not ask for your contacts, photos, location, camera or microphone.

Sharing your result

The share button copies a few lines of emoji (your moves on the daily puzzle, your stars and your recent streak) to your clipboard. In some browsers it also opens your device's share menu. It contains no personal information, and nothing is posted anywhere unless you choose to share or paste it.

Children

Tumblebloom is made for players of all ages, including children. On Android, the first time the game starts it asks for your year of birth. The answer stays on your device and is never sent to us; it only decides how ads are requested (see "Players under 13" above). There is no chat, no account and no way to share personal information inside the game.

We don't collect personal information from anyone, and no player of any age receives personalised ads from us. Purchases need the Google Play account on the device, and parents can require approval for purchases with Google Play's parental controls or Google Family Link. If you're a parent with a question, or you think a child's information has reached us in some way, please contact us and we'll help.

Your choices

  • Change consent: gear on the map, then Privacy choices (where Google offers the form).
  • Reset or delete your advertising ID: in your Android settings, under Google, then Ads.
  • Delete your progress: uninstall the app, or clear the site data in your browser. This also deletes the year of birth.
  • Avoid ads entirely: buy Remove ads on Android (optional reward ads stay available if you want them), or skip every optional ad. The game never requires one.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR, UK GDPR, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act or US state laws), you may have the right to access, correct or delete personal data and to object to its use. Because we don't hold any data about you, requests about ad data are best sent to Google through its privacy tools. You're still welcome to contact us with any question, and we'll answer it.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts handling data differently, we'll update this page and the date at the top before the change ships.
